Important notes and Precautions

General Information about this document

All explanations, test procedures and thresholds have been validated for Bosch components. For the non-Bosch components (e. g. relays, turbo charger, etc.), the values are only indicative. An occurrence for the described symptoms may also be caused by non-Bosch component. Therefore this document does not claim to be a complete vehicle diagnostics handbook. The engine and/or the vehicle manufacture take the responsibility for the complete engine/vehicle diagnosis.
All the pictures are only for the illustration of the component description and the basic test procedure. Other types of project specific components or non-Bosch components may be mounted.
Ensure that during every test the Pins at the electronic control unit or the component plugs not be damaged and use fit adapters for all electric, hydraulic and pneumatic tests.

Safety instructions

When working on vehicles, trailers and their components, please pay comprehensive attention to the safety instructions and damage warnings.

General safety instructions

For safety reasons, vehicles, trailers and their components should only be repaired, serviced and maintained by specially qualified and trained personnel in appropriately equipped and authorized workshops. Attention should always be paid to the applicable version of the industrial safety and environmental protection regulations prescribed by law, accident prevention regulations, technical stipulations, standards and manufacturer's instructions when performing work.

Work on high-pressure fuel system

For all work on the high-pressure fuel system, its surrounding area and the associated components, the manufacturer-specific instructions relating to the systems fitted must always be followed. National legislation, supplementary regulations, guidelines and standards are not contained in these instructions, but must always be heeded as well. Work on the high-pressure fuel system is only to be performed by suitably qualified or trained personnel. Fuel escaping under high pressure may cause serious injuries to the skin and eyes.
A high fire risk is associated with the fuel emerging under high pressure, which may be ignited on when contacting with hot engine components or the exhaust unit.
Safety measure(s):
  • Wear the required personal protective gear for all activities, e.g. protective goggles, protective clothing etc.
  • Always use the protection equipment specified for a particular activity.
  • Personal protective gear, special protection equipment and all necessary safety equipment must meet the applicable requirements, be fit for purpose and be used properly.
  • At the start of, during and until the completion of work, suitable, effective and adequate fire prevention measures must be taken. This also applies to adjacent workplaces and areas.
  • Personnel present in the hazard area and adjacent areas must be instructed in the applicable fire prevention measures.

  • Before testing and replacing components on the high pressure fuel side, the residual fuel pressure must always be completely reduced first.
  • Work on the high-pressure fuel system is only to be performed with the engine cold. This applies in particular to the disconnection of fuel-carrying pipes and components.
  • Fuel lines, their adaptations and any fuel carrying components may not be damaged in any way. If components are faulty, damaged or beyond repair, do not commission the associated system. Damaged parts should be replaced with new ones.
  • If possible, remove ignition key when performing testing/adjustment work. Otherwise, take care to avoid contact with live parts.
  • Persons with pacemakers may not enter the hazard area of high voltage systems or high voltage controlled components, e.g. solenoid valves.
  • Use only original electrical splices.
  • For testing purposes, the electrical splices must have the same properties, e.g. line length, line cross-section and shielding.

Raising vehicles for repair purposes

Only approved and suitable lifting equipment should be used when raising and supporting vehicles and trailers for repairing work. The lifting equipment (e.g. lifting platform, mobile jack), the supports (e.g. blocks) and the safety devices must be appropriate to the load involved and in a reliable safe condition. The equipment must exhibit the relevant safety and approval marks.
Attention must be paid to the technical documentation and accident prevention regulations from the lifting equipment manufacturer when raising the vehicle. All work should be performed by trained personnel. The load bearing elements (e.g. lifting platform supporting arms) should only be positioned at the locations specified by the vehicle manufacturer in the owner's manual or the workshop documentation. Proper and safe positioning of the load bearing elements must be checked and ensured before raising the vehicle.

Parking and securing vehicles for repair purposes

Vehicles and trailers which do not need to be raised for repairing purpose must be parked on a suitable flat surface. The parking-brake system must be applied to stop the vehicle from rolling away. For vehicles with automatic transmission, the selector lever must additionally be set to <P>. If the above action cannot be taken on account of repair work, rolling of the vehicle in either direction must be prevented by blocking at least two wheels with suitable chocks. Suitable action must be taken to prevent unintentional start-up of the vehicle.

Working in inspection pits under vehicles

Inspection and working beneath vehicles with floor pits must comply with the requirements of various orders, guidelines and regulations. Furthermore they must be approved and accepted for such purposes. This also applies to lifting equipment and under floor pits. Before using, it is essential to heed the manufacturer's technical documentation, the appropriate safety instructions and the generally valid accident prevention regulations.
  • Inspection pits, under floor pits and the corresponding safety devices must be appropriate to the application and be in a reliable safe condition. Also, the size must be fitted with the vehicle.
  • Only the equipment with relevant safety and approval marks should be used.
  • Work in inspection pits and under floor pits should only be performed by trained personnel.
  • Non-slip steps should be used for the entrance of the inspection pits and under floor pits.
  • When inspection pits and under floor pits must be secured with cover when not used. Make sure no falling down into the opening.
  • For repairing purposes, vehicles and trailers should only be raised and supported by suitable lifting equipment approved for this purpose. Refer to safety instructions on <Raising vehicles for repair purposes>.
  • Certain gases are heavier than air and may accumulate at the bottom of inspection and under floor pits. This may lead to an increased danger of asphyxiation and/or fire. It is essential to compliance with the appropriate safety precautions.

Exhaust gases

Exhaust gases from internal combustion engines and auxiliary units, such as independent heaters (auxiliary heaters), contain toxic component which can lead to serious health hazard and even death in extreme cases. In enclosed areas, internal combustion engines and independent heaters are therefore only to be operated in conjunction with a suitable, fully functional exhaust gas extractor approved for such purposes. The extractor must be properly connected and switched on before starting up the engine.

Rotating parts

There is a danger of being trapped or injured on contact with rotating parts during troubleshooting or vehicle assembly work, particularly in the engine compartment. Hazards may result from the automatic start-up of auxiliary components (e.g. radiator fan). When working in the engine compartment, suitable action must be taken to reliably guard against start-up of the engine and its components.

Hot parts

The skin may be burned if contacting with the surface of hot components during troubleshooting or vehicle assembly work. Particularly, attention should be paid when working with the engine compartment, the exhaust muffler system and the wheel brake.

Flammable substances (e. g.: Diesel, Gasoline, cleaning fluids)

When working on vehicles and the components (even when removed), there will be different types of flammable substances with varying composition. This may lead to a considerable risk of fire and explosion.

Further information:
  • Suitable and reliable measures must be taken before disassembling pressed pipes and components filled with flammable substances.
  • The escaping substances must be collected immediately and stored in suitable approved containers before proper disposal.
  • Soaked cloths and binders must be removed immediately from the work area and stored in suitable approved containers before proper disposal.
  • Before working on the vehicle, suitable measures must be taken, e.g. battery disconnection and/or the use of non spark-drawing tools to prevent sources of ignition.
  • All types of thermal operation, such as soldering and/or welding work, parting and/or grinding work, should not be performed on one object simultaneously.
  • Reliable and suitable measures must be taken to shield from spark.
  • Adequate and suitable ventilation measures should be taken for reliably prevent the fire, explosion, excessively concentration levels. Additional measures, such as the electrostatic discharge and potential equalization, may also be needed.
  • During working, suitable, effective and adequate fire prevention measures must be taken.
  • Flammable substances should only be stored in suitable approved containers. The applicable storage specifications, regulations and stipulations must be heeded and observed.

Cleanliness when handling diesel systems

The diesel injection system components are made of high-precision parts which are subjected to extreme loads. On account of this high precision, absolute cleanliness is to be ensured when working on the fuel system, as even dirt particles as small as 0.2mm may lead to the failure of components and thus to engine damage.

Observe the following rules before starting work on the fuel system:
  • The fuel system must be closed.
  • Visually inspect fuel system for leakage and damage.
  • The engine must be dry and clean when starting work on the fuel system.
  • Clean engine and engine compartment.
  • If use is made of a steam cleaner, fit covers over electrical components.
  • Never aim steam cleaner directly at electrical components.
  • Drying with compressed air is only permitted with the fuel system closed.
  • Use new, clean sheeting to cover areas of the engine compartment from which dirt particles could flake off.
  • Before starting work, the workplace should be cleaned and as far as possible completely free of dust.
  • Take care to avoid currents of air which stir up dust.
    • Grinding and welding work.
    • Brake repairs.
    • Starting engines.
    • Use of ventilation and heating systems.
    • Use of ventilation systems and dynamometers.
  • Always wear clean overalls when working on the fuel system.
  • Exclusive use is to be made of lint free cloths for cleaning the fuel system.
  • Clean tools and equipment before starting work.
  • Never use tools exhibiting signs of damage (e.g. cracks in chrome coating).
  • Work on components removed is only to be performed at an appropriately equipped workplace.
Observe the following rules when working on the open fuel system:
  • Never use old cleaning or test fluid for cleaning purposes.
  • Compressed air is not to be used for cleaning. Use suitable extractors (industrial vacuum cleaner) to remove loose dirt particles such as flakes of paint and insulating material.
  • Never use materials such as cloths, cardboard or wood when removing and installing components, as these could lose particles and fiber.
  • Following removal, immediately seal the openings in the components with appropriate clean caps.
  • Always keep an assortment of sealing caps to hand (in a suitable tray).
  • Sealing caps should be stored in their original wrapping to protect against dust and dirt. Dispose of caps after once-only use.
  • Components removed are to be stored carefully in a clean, sealed container.
  • Leave new components in their original wrapping until immediately prior to use. Always use the original wrapping of the new component when despatching parts removed.

Usage of testers

When using testers, it is essential to heed the technical documentation of the manufacturer and in particular the safety instructions.

General information:
  • The testers used must be appropriate to and approved for the intended application.
  • The testers and the associated safety devices must be in a reliable safe condition.
  • The relevant safety and approval marks should be exhibited on the equipment.
  • Working with testers should only be performed by trained personnel.

Basic requirement for diagnosis

  • Charging status of Battery is OK.
  • All plug-in connections are firmly attached and not interchanged. This is particularly important in the case of crash vehicles, previous repairs and retrofitting work.
  • Ground connection(s) of engine, body and component <Engine control unit> is OK.
  • Other systems which may influence the engine management are OK:
    • e.g. with respect to:
      • Air conditioning system
      • Transmission control
      • Immobilizer
      • Engine in proper mechanical working order.
    • E.g. with respect to:
      • Valve timing and valve clearance.
      • Cylinder compression/pressure loss.
      • Leakage of the exhaust unit.
      • Leakage of the intake system.
      • Crankshaft housing breather.
  • Level and quality of fuel, engine oil and coolant is OK and in line with vehicle manufacturer's specifications.
  • Qualified components are used for this vehicle/system during previous repair or service work.
  • No power-enhancing modifications made on engines (particularly chip tuning and/or changes to valve timing).
